# Cover Letters

Use this reference only when reviewing or helping shape a cover letter. A cover letter is a concise, role-specific argument that connects selected candidate evidence to the employer's stated needs. It should add context or motivation that the CV does not already communicate, not retell the CV or the candidate's life story.

## Core Structure

### Opening

The opening should quickly establish the application context and a credible reason to continue reading.

- Name the role and connect the candidate to a specific aspect of the organization, role, mission, product, or stated challenge.
- Base company observations on the posting or other user-supplied evidence. Do not invent a company problem, strategy, culture, or priority.
- Connect that context to one relevant capability or piece of experience.
- Avoid empty openings such as "I am writing to express my interest" when the same space can establish specific alignment.
- Mention a referral early only when it is genuine, the candidate has permission to use the person's name, and it helps explain the connection.

An evidence-backed problem-solution opening can work when the employer has clearly stated a need or challenge. Treat it as one available structure, not a universally superior formula.

### Body

Use the body to prove selected points of fit rather than list traits or summarize every role.

- Choose one or two examples that address the employer's most important criteria.
- Give enough context to understand the situation, the candidate's action, and the result.
- Prefer outcomes, scope, scale, frequency, or constraints when they are known and material.
- Use numbers only when they are true and meaningful. Never manufacture a metric because quantified prose appears stronger.
- Explain relevance explicitly; do not expect the reader to infer why an achievement matters for this role.
- Use terminology from the posting only where it accurately describes the evidence.
- Short bullets can make distinct evidence easier to scan, but use them only when they suit the application channel and do not duplicate the CV.

### Closing

The closing should complete the argument without becoming passive, presumptive, or repetitive.

- Reinforce the strongest point of alignment or motivation in one sentence.
- Express interest in discussing the role or next step.
- Avoid demands, exaggerated enthusiasm, generic thanks, or a full summary of the letter.

## Specificity And Voice

- Each paragraph should contain a concrete detail that could not be pasted unchanged into an unrelated application.
- Prefer natural language the candidate would plausibly use in a professional conversation.
- Remove generic traits and stock phrases unless evidence immediately gives them meaning.
- Vary sentence structure when repetition makes the prose mechanical, but do not introduce variation for its own sake.
- AI may help organize or critique material, subject to the active agent's boundaries. The candidate must verify every claim and retain ownership of the final voice.
- Do not claim that wording can evade or reliably pass AI-detection software. Evaluate generic or artificial-sounding prose by its likely reader effect instead.

## Length And Presentation

- Keep the letter only as long as needed for its opening, selected evidence, and closing. Three to five short paragraphs is a useful shape, not a universal rule.
- Make the role, organization, candidate identity, contact details, and reading order clear.
- Keep critical content in selectable, extractable text. Check that exported files are not image-only and that decorative elements do not interrupt reading order.
- Use a conventional file type accepted by the application process and follow any stated submission instructions over general advice.
- Keep titles, dates, employer names, qualifications, and other claims consistent with the CV or resume.

## Review Checks

Ask these questions before recommending sentence-level edits:

1. Does the letter establish specific, evidenced alignment in the opening?
2. Does each body example support an important role criterion?
3. Does the letter add useful context rather than repeat the CV?
4. Are all claims, metrics, company observations, and referrals supportable?
5. Does the prose sound direct and specific rather than generic or over-polished?
6. Is the next step clear without assuming an interview or decision?
7. Will the intended submission channel preserve readable, extractable text?

Report structural and evidence problems before polishing individual sentences. A fluent letter with weak or unsupported alignment is still a weak application document.
